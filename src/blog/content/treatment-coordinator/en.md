> A **treatment coordinator** links the doctor and the patient for treatments that need a big decision, such as dental implants or cosmetic surgery. Their job is to explain the options in simple terms, answer concerns, organize the practical steps and follow up until the patient decides, **without pressure** and without unrealistic promises.

## Why does a clinic need a treatment coordinator?

Higher-cost treatments are rarely decided in a single message. The patient compares, consults their family and needs someone who answers patiently. The doctor usually lacks time for this follow-up, and the receptionist is busy with daily appointments. That is where the coordinator comes in.

## What does a treatment coordinator do?

- Reviews the patient's situation after the [initial qualification](/en/blog/turn-inquiries-into-bookings).
- Explains the treatment options the doctor has set out, with the benefits and limits of each.
- Explains cost, payment options and expected duration transparently.
- Organizes the practical side for patients coming from another city or country, such as consecutive appointments and accommodation.
- Follows up after the consultation until the patient decides, and again after treatment to check on them.

## Skills that make a good coordinator

1. **Listening:** understanding what truly worries the patient before talking about treatment.
2. **Knowledge:** understanding procedures well enough to explain them accurately, while referring medical questions to the doctor.
3. **Honesty:** never exaggerating results or downplaying recovery time.
4. **Organization:** logging every contact in the [CRM](/en/blog/clinic-crm-system) and knowing when the next follow-up is due.
5. **Empathy:** treating an important health and financial decision with respect.

## Treatment coordination versus pushy selling

Pushy selling aims to close at any cost. It may work once, but it hurts the clinic's reputation and brings [negative reviews](/en/blog/clinic-reviews-reputation). Treatment coordination aims to help the patient choose what suits them, even if they decide to wait. A patient who feels respected comes back and recommends the clinic.

Q: Is a treatment coordinator a doctor?
A: Not necessarily. The coordinator explains, organizes and follows up, while diagnosis and the treatment plan remain the doctor's responsibility.

Q: Is a treatment coordinator useful for every specialty?
A: They are most useful for treatments that need a big decision or several sessions, such as dental implants and orthodontics, aesthetics and hair transplants.

Q: Can a coordinator work remotely?
A: Yes. Much of the contact happens over WhatsApp and phone calls, especially with patients coming from another city or country.
