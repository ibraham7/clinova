> A good FAQ page saves your team time and reassures the patient before they call, and it is one of the most useful kinds of content for search because people **type their questions exactly as they are** into Google and AI tools. The secret is that the questions are real and the answers direct and honest.

## Where do the questions come from?

Don't invent them. Collect them from:

- **Conversations and calls:** what keeps repeating? Look through WhatsApp messages for repeated questions.
- **Reception staff:** they hear questions every day.
- **Social media comments** and people's questions under posts.
- **Patients' objections** that came up while [following up inquiries](/en/blog/price-objections-patient-hesitation).

## Questions that suit a dental clinic

- How long does the first visit take and what happens in it?
- Is treatment painful, and how do you deal with fear?
- What are the payment methods and are there installments?
- How do I book, and what if I need to reschedule?
- What should I bring with me?
- What are the opening hours and how do I get there?
- What should I do if pain appears after treatment?

## How to write the answer

1. **Start with the answer** in the first sentence, then add details.
2. **Write as you would speak to the patient:** without complicated terms.
3. **Be as specific as possible:** an approximate duration, not just "it depends on the case".
4. **Link to the next step:** a booking link or WhatsApp.
5. **Update the page** when prices, hours or services change.

## What not to say

- Don't give a general diagnosis that suggests it applies to everyone. State that the decision comes after an exam.
- Don't promise guaranteed results.
- Don't copy answers from other sites: duplication hurts visibility and trust.
- Don't include numbers or statistics you can't prove.

## Appearing in search and AI tools

- Give each question a clear heading phrased as a question, with the answer beneath it.
- Put the questions on one organized page, or at the end of each service page. You can use FAQ structured data, as our own site does.
- Write in your patients' languages if you serve them, as in [patients from abroad](/en/blog/medical-tourism-patient-inquiries).
- Make the page fast and easy to read on a phone, as in [your clinic's website](/en/blog/clinic-website-turn-visitors-into-inquiries).

Q: How many questions should I put on the page?
A: Start with ten to fifteen real questions and add more as new ones appear. Quality matters more than quantity.

Q: Should I write detailed medical answers?
A: Give useful general information and make clear that diagnosis and the plan come after an exam, and leave precise medical details to the doctor.

Q: How often should I update the page?
A: Review it every three months and update it immediately if a price, opening hours or a service changes.
