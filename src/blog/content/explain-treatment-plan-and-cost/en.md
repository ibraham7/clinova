> A patient who doesn't understand the treatment plan won't accept it, however good it is. The rule here: **explain the plan in short steps, offer one or two options, itemize the cost clearly and let the patient ask questions**. That way they decide with understanding, and there is less hesitation and fewer surprises later.

## Why do patients hesitate after the exam?

- They don't understand why they need all this treatment.
- They don't know how much they will pay in the end.
- They fear pain, time or the number of visits.
- They want to compare with another clinic.

## How to order the explanation

1. **The current situation:** what the doctor saw in simple language, with a photo or X-ray if possible.
2. **What needs treatment first:** whatever hurts or is getting worse comes before the rest.
3. **The options:** if there is more than one route, explain the difference in time, durability and price. Don't present one option as if it were the only one.
4. **Duration and visits:** how many visits and how long each takes.
5. **After treatment:** follow-up and care.

## How to present the cost

- **Itemize it:** the price of each procedure instead of a vague total.
- **Say what the price doesn't include:** any visits or materials that might be added.
- **Mention possible changes:** if something comes up during treatment it will be discussed with them before going ahead.
- **Offer payment options:** installments if the clinic provides them, without pressure.
- **Write the offer down:** a message or file with the details that the patient can come back to and share with family.

## What not to do

- Don't use frightening phrases to push the patient into a decision.
- Don't promise results you can't guarantee.
- Don't hide the cost until the end.
- Don't pressure them to decide in the same session.

## After the session

Send a written summary of the plan and cost the same day and tell them when you'll follow up. If hesitation about price appears, see [how to handle price objections](/en/blog/price-objections-patient-hesitation). Q: Should I give the patient one option or several?
A: If real options exist, present them with the difference between them, because patients decide more confidently when they see alternatives.

Q: Should I give the full price in the first session?
A: If possible after the exam, and if the estimate depends on what appears during treatment, say so clearly.

Q: What is the best way to send the plan?
A: A written message or file after the session, because patients usually review it and share it with family before deciding.
