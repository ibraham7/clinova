> When a patient says "it's too expensive", they usually don't mean the number alone. It may mean they don't understand what they are paying for, don't trust you yet, or can't pay in one go. The answer is not an instant discount but **to understand the reason first and then answer it honestly**.

## The real reasons behind "expensive"

- **They don't see the value:** nobody explained what the price includes or how you differ from others.
- **They don't trust yet:** they need more reassurance about the doctor or the result.
- **Ability to pay:** the amount is too big in one payment.
- **They are comparing:** they saw a lower price elsewhere without knowing what it covers.
- **They're not in a hurry:** they postpone the decision and use the price as an excuse.

## Step one: ask before you defend

A simple phrase such as "I understand. Do you mean the amount is more than your budget, or are you comparing it with something specific?" reveals the reason without putting the patient on the defensive.

## Respond according to the reason

- **If it's value:** explain again what the price includes and why the method and materials were chosen, plus the number of visits and follow-up. See [how to explain a treatment plan](/en/blog/explain-treatment-plan-and-cost).
- **If it's trust:** show real photos, the doctor's qualifications and patient stories with their consent, without exaggeration.
- **If it's ability to pay:** offer splitting the treatment into phases by priority, or installments if the clinic provides them.
- **If it's a comparison:** ask to see the other quotes and compare what each one includes together. Don't criticize competitors.

## What not to do

- Don't cut the price immediately, because it suggests the first price was inflated.
- Don't frighten them with complications to push them into accepting.
- Don't promise results you can't guarantee.
- Don't pressure them, because pressure sends them somewhere else.

## When to let them think

If the patient needs time, respect it. Say: "Take your time. I'll send you the plan in writing, and I can check in with you in two days if you like." Then actually follow up. A patient who wasn't pressured often comes back.

## After a refusal

Ask gently whether anything could change, and note the reason in their file. After a while you can contact them through [reactivating past patients](/en/blog/reactivate-past-patients).

Q: Should I lower the price if the patient objects?
A: Don't start with a discount. Understand the reason first, because the problem may be trust or the way of paying rather than the number.

Q: What should I do if they compare my price with another clinic?
A: Ask for the details of the other offer and compare what each includes, without disparaging the competitor.

Q: How many times should I follow up a patient who hesitated over price?
A: One or two gentle follow-ups, then leave the door open. Insisting damages the relationship.
