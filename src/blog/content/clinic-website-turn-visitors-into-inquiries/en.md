> A good clinic website doesn't aim only to look nice. It aims to **make the visitor understand what you offer, trust you and know how to reach you within seconds**. Visitors are usually lost for simple reasons that can be fixed: a vague offer, a hidden button or a slow site on mobile.

## What is the visitor looking for?

They want quick answers to three questions: **Do you offer what I need? Can I trust you? How do I get in touch or book?** Every page should answer them.

## Page elements that convert visitors

1. **A clear headline at the top:** what you offer, for whom and where. For example "Dental clinic in Istanbul: implants and cosmetic care".
2. **A clear contact button:** WhatsApp or booking, visible without the visitor having to hunt for it, and repeated down the page.
3. **Services clearly laid out:** each with a short description and a link to its details.
4. **Trust:** real photos of the team and the clinic, the doctors' qualifications, real reviews, an address and a phone number.
5. **Frequently asked questions:** they answer objections before the visitor asks them.
6. **One next step:** don't offer ten different options.

## Mobile first

Most visitors arrive from a phone, so make sure the site:

- Opens quickly and isn't slowed down by large images.
- Has readable text and buttons that are easy to tap.
- Opens WhatsApp or a call with one tap.

## Languages

If your patients come from different countries, show the site in their languages and don't rely on unreviewed machine translation. See [handling patients from abroad](/en/blog/medical-tourism-patient-inquiries).

## How to know where visitors are lost

- How many visitors came, and how many of them clicked a contact button?
- Where did they come from: an ad, search or social media?
- Which pages do they leave quickly?

Record each patient's source to know which channel brings real patients, as in [marketing numbers](/en/blog/clinic-marketing-metrics-cost-per-patient).

## After the click

A visitor who clicked the WhatsApp button has become an inquiry. If the reply is slow they are lost, so link the site to a [fast, organized way of replying](/en/blog/turn-inquiries-into-bookings).

Q: What is the most important element on a clinic website?
A: A clear, easy contact button, because the best site is useless if the visitor doesn't know how to get in touch.

Q: Do I need a blog?
A: A blog helps you appear in search if it truly answers patients' questions, but it isn't a substitute for a clear services page.

Q: How often should I review the website?
A: Review the numbers monthly, test the site yourself from your phone from time to time, and make sure the buttons and numbers work.
