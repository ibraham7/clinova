> The most important number for clinic marketing is not clicks or followers but **the cost of a booked patient who actually showed up**. To get there you need only four numbers: how many inquiries came in, how many were booked, how many attended, and how much you spent. Everything else is detail that helps you improve.

## Why ad numbers alone aren't enough

Ad platforms show clicks, impressions and messages, and those numbers stop before the patient walks into your clinic. A campaign can look excellent and bring many messages while nobody books because replies were slow or the audience was wrong. That is why you need to measure **what happens after the message**, which is what really matters to you.

## The core numbers

1. **Inquiries:** everyone who contacted you from an ad, the website or a referral.
2. **Qualified inquiries:** people who want a treatment your clinic offers and live in an area you serve.
3. **Bookings:** how many of them made an appointment.
4. **Attendance:** how many actually came. [Reducing no-shows](/en/blog/reduce-no-shows-whatsapp-reminders) raises this number.
5. **Spend:** what you paid on each channel in the same period.

## How to calculate the cost per patient

- **Cost per inquiry** = spend on the channel ÷ number of inquiries from it.
- **Cost per booking** = spend ÷ number of bookings.
- **Cost per attended patient** = spend ÷ number of patients who attended.

A made-up example, for explanation only: if you spend 1,000 on a channel and get 50 inquiries, 10 bookings and 8 attended patients, the cost per inquiry is 20, per booking 100 and per attended patient 125. Compare these numbers across your own channels, not with general figures from the internet.

## Measure the conversion between stages

Look at where patients are lost: between inquiry and reply, between reply and booking, or between booking and attendance. Each stage has a different fix: faster replies, a clearer offer, or an appointment reminder. This is what patient stages in a [CRM](/en/blog/clinic-crm-system) show you.

## The most common mistake: not knowing the patient's source

Without recording each patient's source you can't calculate anything. Make "How did you hear about us?" a fixed part of booking and record the answer. Combine it with ad tracking as described in [attracting patients](/en/blog/clinic-patient-acquisition).

## What not to compare against

- Don't compare with "market average" percentages without knowing where they come from: specialties, cities and seasons differ.
- Don't judge a campaign after a few days, because some patients decide after weeks.
- Don't forget the patient's value after treatment: returning and recommending are part of the return.

## A suggested review rhythm

- **Weekly:** unanswered inquiries and new bookings.
- **Monthly:** cost per channel, attendance rate and the reasons patients are lost.
- **Every three months:** which channel to spend more on and which to stop.

Q: What is the most important number to follow if I have no time?
A: The cost of an attended patient for each channel, because it combines spend and result in one figure.

Q: How long should I wait before judging an ad campaign?
A: There is no fixed period. Wait until enough data has gathered and the decision period of patients in your specialty has passed, and don't change the campaign every day.

Q: Do I need special software for these numbers?
A: You can start with a simple spreadsheet, but as inquiries multiply, a system that records source and stage automatically is more accurate and saves time.
