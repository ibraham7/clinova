> Most clinics don't suffer from too few inquiries as much as from losing them. Turning an inquiry into an appointment depends on five steps: **replying quickly**, **qualifying the patient** to understand their need, **answering their concerns** clearly, **proposing one specific next step**, and **organized follow-up** for those who haven't decided yet.

## 1. Reply fast

An interested patient contacts more than one clinic. Set a clear response-time target during working hours, and use [AI replies on WhatsApp](/en/blog/whatsapp-ai-for-clinics) outside them so no question goes unanswered.

## 2. Qualify before quoting a price

"How much does it cost?" is usually the start of the conversation, not the end. Before answering, find out:

- Which treatment do they actually need?
- Do they have a previous diagnosis or X-rays?
- What worries them most: cost, pain or the result?
- When do they want to start?

Qualifying helps you give an accurate answer and shows the patient that the clinic cares about their case, not just about selling.

## 3. Answer concerns before they are raised

The recurring concerns are well known: pain, cost, duration, guarantees and the doctor's experience. Prepare clear, honest answers, and support them with a short video from the doctor or with real patient stories shared with consent.

## 4. Propose one specific step

Don't end the conversation with "contact us when you decide". Offer a clear step: a free consultation, an examination on a specific day, or a short call with the treatment coordinator. A concrete option is easier to accept than an open decision.

## 5. Follow up regularly without being pushy

Many bookings come after the second or third follow-up. Set a simple schedule:

1. A follow-up the day after the inquiry.
2. Another a few days later with useful information, not just an offer.
3. A final follow-up two weeks later asking whether they are still interested.

Log all of it in a [CRM](/en/blog/clinic-crm-system) so follow-up never depends on anyone's memory.

## Measure every stage

Know the number of inquiries, the share that were qualified, the share that booked and the share that attended. Once you see where patients drop off, you know exactly what to improve.

Q: What is the main reason inquiries are lost?
A: Slow replies and no follow-up. Many patients don't say no; they get busy and forget if nobody contacts them again.

Q: Should I state the price in the first message?
A: It's best to give an approximate range once you understand the case, and explain that the final price is set after the examination. Vague answers push patients toward another clinic.

Q: How many times should I follow up with a patient who hasn't booked?
A: Usually two to three times over two weeks, as long as each follow-up adds real value and you respect the patient's wish if they ask you to stop.
