> Many patients write in the evening or on holidays, when your clinic is closed. Someone who writes and receives no answer **starts looking at another clinic within minutes**. The solution isn't for your team to stay online all night but a clear automatic message followed by organized follow-up in the morning.

## What does the patient expect?

They don't expect a doctor to reply at 2 a.m. They expect to know their message arrived and to know **when** someone will answer. That is enough to keep them with you.

## The automatic welcome message

Keep it short and include:

1. Thanks for getting in touch and a line about the clinic.
2. Working hours and the expected reply time: "We'll reply tomorrow before 10 a.m."
3. What they can do now: a booking link or one question that helps the team, such as "Which service are you interested in?"
4. A number for emergencies, if the clinic handles them.

Don't promise what you can't deliver, such as "we'll reply in minutes" while closed.

## What the automatic message must not do

- Don't give a diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Don't ignore emergencies: tell the patient what to do if their pain is severe.
- Don't sound like a cold machine: a friendly tone is far better.

## Follow-up in the morning

- Assign someone to open the **night's message list first** every morning.
- Sort it: emergencies, then people who want to book, then general questions.
- Every patient waiting for an answer should get it at the time you promised. That is where [inquiries turn into bookings](/en/blog/turn-inquiries-into-bookings).

## When to consider smart replies

AI can answer repeated questions at night, such as location, hours and services, and collect the patient's details so the team finds them ready. But leave medical questions and exact prices to the team, and see the article on [AI replies](/en/blog/whatsapp-ai-for-clinics).

Q: Do I need an automatic message if my team replies until evening?
A: Yes, because some patients write after hours or on holidays, and the message reassures them and sets a reply time.

Q: How long should I wait before replying to a night message?
A: The first hour of the morning shift. The later you reply, the more likely the patient has chosen another clinic.

Q: Should I send the welcome message to everyone who writes?
A: Once per new conversation, or after a long gap, is enough. Repeating it with every message is annoying.
