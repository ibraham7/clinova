> Patient data is among the most sensitive things a clinic holds. The simplest practical rule is **the least access that gets the job done**: each employee sees and edits only what their role needs. This doesn't mean distrusting the team. It protects the patient, the employee and the clinic from mistakes and unintended use.

## Why isn't "we trust everyone" enough?

- **Mistakes happen:** an employee deletes a record or changes an invoice by accident.
- **Curiosity:** looking up the file of someone you know is not part of the job.
- **Teams change:** people leave and others join, and wide-open permissions stay open.
- **Accountability:** if something goes wrong you need to know who did what.

## A suggested split of roles

- **Reception:** appointments, contact details and conversations, without sensitive medical details or financial reports.
- **Doctors:** medical records and treatments for the patients they treat, without the clinic's financial settings.
- **Accounting:** invoices and payments, without clinical notes.
- **Sales team or treatment coordinator:** inquiries, patient stages and follow-up, as in the [treatment coordinator's role](/en/blog/treatment-coordinator).
- **Management:** reports, settings and user management.

Every clinic works differently, so adjust the split to your size, but write it down clearly.

## Conversations are sensitive data too

WhatsApp conversations contain names, photos and sometimes health details. So ask yourself:

- Who can read a patient's conversation?
- Can a conversation be assigned to a specific employee?
- Does the person replying know a colleague already replied?
- Are there internal notes that never reach the patient? They reduce the need to share information outside the system.

## Habits that protect your data

1. **One account per employee:** never share one password between people.
2. **Strong passwords:** long and not reused, changed whenever someone leaves.
3. **Remove access immediately** when an employee's work ends.
4. **Regular backups** and a test that you can restore them.
5. **Don't send patient data** through personal apps or screenshots of the system.
6. **Follow the data-protection laws** that apply in your country, and consult a legal professional when needed.

## A simple periodic review

Every three months: pull the list of users, remove anyone who no longer works there and check whether each person's role still fits. You can ask: who can see [patient conversations](/en/blog/clinic-crm-system) right now, and do they really need to?

Q: Should the owner see everything?
A: Usually yes, but even the owner benefits from a personal account and a record of who did what.

Q: What should I do when an employee leaves the clinic?
A: Disable their account immediately, change any password they knew, and reassign their conversations and tasks to a colleague.

Q: Is a strong password enough protection?
A: It is an important step but not enough on its own. You also need suitable permissions, backups and regular reviews.
