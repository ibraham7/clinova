> A medical-tourism patient decides slowly, compares several clinics and usually writes on WhatsApp at any hour. The clinic that **replies quickly, in their language, with a clear offer that explains what happens step by step**, gets ahead before prices are even compared. All it takes is a simple way of organizing inquiries and follow-up.

## What makes these patients different?

- **They judge trust before price:** they want to know who the doctor is, how experienced they are and what happens when they arrive.
- **They ask several clinics at once:** whoever answers first and most clearly wins the conversation.
- **They can't come in for an exam:** so they rely on photos, reports and chat.
- **They are planning a trip:** they need dates, accommodation, transport and the number of visits.

## Step one: reply in minutes, not hours

[Reply speed](/en/blog/turn-inquiries-into-bookings) decides a lot. If your team can't answer around the clock, set up a welcome message that thanks them and says when they will hear back, and make one person responsible for following up first thing in the morning.

## Language and time zones

- Answer in the patient's language: Arabic, English, Turkish or French depending on their country, and don't rely on machine translation without review for anything medical.
- Always state times together with the **time zone** to avoid misunderstandings.
- Keep pre-reviewed message templates for the most common questions: duration, number of visits, accommodation, payment methods.

## What to collect before giving a price

1. Name, country of residence and preferred language.
2. What they want in their own words: a new smile, implants, braces...
3. Clear photos of the teeth, or X-rays if available.
4. Health history, medications and allergies.
5. The period when they can travel.

Don't give a final diagnosis remotely. Offer a **preliminary estimate that depends on an in-person exam**, and explain what could change.

## How to write an offer that builds trust

- The treatment steps, the number of visits and how long each one takes.
- What the price includes and what it doesn't, with no surprises later.
- Information about the doctor and the clinic with real photos and no exaggeration.
- What happens after treatment: follow-up, and what to do if a problem appears after they return home.

Don't promise what you can't guarantee, and don't use other patients' results without their explicit consent.

## Follow-up before travel and after returning home

- **Before arrival:** a confirmation message with the details, then a reminder before the trip about the documents needed and the clinic's address.
- **During the visit:** record everything that was done in the patient's file.
- **After returning home:** a check-in message a few days later, another after a while, then a [review request](/en/blog/clinic-reviews-reputation) if they are happy.

Q: Can I give a final price before the patient visits the clinic?
A: You can give a preliminary estimate, but it stays subject to an in-person exam because the case may differ from what the photos show.

Q: How do I follow up with a patient who stopped replying?
A: Send a short message after two or three days offering help, then another a week later. If they still don't reply, leave the door open without pushing.

Q: Do I need a translator?
A: A team member who speaks the patient's language helps, and for anything medical you should review any machine translation before sending it.
