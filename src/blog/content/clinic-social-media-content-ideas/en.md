> A clinic's social media content is built on **trust before selling**: useful information, real faces and answers to questions patients really ask. It doesn't need a huge budget, but **a simple schedule the team sticks to** and clear limits on what may be posted in the medical field.

## What is the content for?

- So people know who you are and where you are.
- So they trust the team before the first visit.
- So they find answers that save them confusion.
- So interest turns into a message or a booking, not just likes.

## Ideas that work for most clinics

1. **A short useful tip:** how to brush well, when to see the dentist, the difference between whitening and cleaning.
2. **Common patient questions:** one question and a brief answer, noting that diagnosis comes after an exam.
3. **Behind the scenes:** sterilizing instruments, preparing the room, a working day. Things that show cleanliness and care.
4. **Meet the team:** the doctor, their experience and interests, and the reception staff.
5. **Patient experiences:** a patient's opinion with their explicit consent, without revealing their health data.
6. **Seasons and occasions:** tips before Ramadan, back to school or summer.
7. **Answers to people's comments:** turning a repeated question into a post.

## The limits of medical advertising

Laws differ between countries, so check what applies in yours. In general:

- **Don't promise guaranteed results** and don't exaggerate.
- **Before and after:** don't publish a patient's photos without their written consent, and respect local restrictions on this type of image.
- **Don't give a diagnosis** in comments or messages.
- **Don't display anyone's health data.**
- **Don't criticize other clinics.**

## How often to post

One or two posts a week, consistently, is better than an intense campaign that stops after two weeks. Pick only two channels you can look after.

Q: Do I need a professional photographer?
A: No. Real photos and videos of good quality from a phone are enough, and may feel closer to patients than very polished content.

Q: Should I post before-and-after photos?
A: Only with the patient's explicit consent and as far as your country's laws allow. Check before posting.

Q: How long should I wait before seeing a result?
A: Content builds trust gradually, so don't judge after a week. Watch inquiries and sources monthly.
