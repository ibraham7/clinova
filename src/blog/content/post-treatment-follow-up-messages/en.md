> Follow-up after treatment is not a courtesy, it is part of care. A short message at the right time **reassures the patient, reminds them of care instructions, catches a small problem before it grows, and builds the trust that brings them back and makes them recommend you**. What matters is that it is organized and human, not a cold automation.

## Why follow up after treatment?

- The patient feels the clinic cares about them after they leave.
- You catch early any pain or symptoms that need a review.
- You remind them of care instructions they often forget.
- You book the review appointment or next session on time.

## A suggested follow-up sequence

1. **The same day:** a short thank-you and a written summary of the care instructions, so they can refer back to it.
2. **A day or two later:** "How are you feeling?" with the patient knowing how to reach you if pain or anything unexpected appears.
3. **Before the review appointment:** an appointment reminder, as in [WhatsApp reminders](/en/blog/reduce-no-shows-whatsapp-reminders).
4. **After treatment ends:** a congratulations message and a [review](/en/blog/clinic-reviews-reputation) request if they are happy.
5. **After a few months:** a reminder for a check-up or cleaning, which is part of [reactivating patients](/en/blog/reactivate-past-patients).

## How to write a warm message

- Use their name and refer to what was done: "Hi Sara, how are you feeling after yesterday's filling?"
- Make it a short open question, not a long text.
- Say how to get in touch if a problem appears, and an emergency number if there is one.
- Don't send one copied message to every patient: change what is specific to them.

## Limits to respect

- Don't give a diagnosis through messages. If the patient describes worrying symptoms, ask them to come in or call, and let the doctor decide.
- Respect the patient's consent to be contacted and the data-privacy laws of your country.
- Don't send sensitive medical information through insecure channels.
- Don't overdo the number of messages.

## Who is responsible?

Assign someone to follow up patients after every treatment, or messages get lost between everyone. In many clinics this is one of the [treatment coordinator's](/en/blog/treatment-coordinator) tasks.

Q: When should I send the first message after treatment?
A: The same day, then a check-in a day or two later.

Q: What should I do if the patient says they have severe pain?
A: Don't give treatment advice through messages. Ask them to call or come in immediately, and inform the doctor.

Q: Should I ask for a review in the first message?
A: It is better to wait until treatment is finished and the patient is reassured, and then ask kindly.
