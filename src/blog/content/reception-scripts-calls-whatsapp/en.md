> The first person a patient speaks to at the clinic is the receptionist, and their first impression forms in **the first three sentences**. You don't need a rigid memorized script, but **a simple structure and ready phrases** that help staff reply quickly, warmly and clearly, and make the whole team speak with one voice.

## The basic rule: listen before you answer

Start with an open question: "How can I help you?" and then listen to the whole answer. Many patients ask about price when they are really worried about pain or want reassurance.

## A four-step reply structure

1. **Greeting and introduction:** "Welcome to [clinic name], this is [your name]."
2. **Understand the need:** "What exactly do you need? Have you visited us before?"
3. **Answer clearly:** specific, honest information.
4. **The next step:** "Shall I book you for tomorrow or next week?"

## Ready-made phrases

- **Answering a call:** "[Clinic name], good morning, [your name] speaking. How can I help?"
- **When asked about price:** "The price depends on your case, and after the exam we'll give you a clear figure. Would you like us to book an exam?" After the exam, see [explaining the plan and cost](/en/blog/explain-treatment-plan-and-cost).
- **When no slot is available:** "The earliest slot is on such a day at such a time. Does that suit you, or shall I put you on the waiting list and call if an earlier time frees up?"
- **When apologizing for a mistake:** "We apologize for that, and we'll take care of it now. Give me a minute to check."
- **On WhatsApp:** a short reply using their name, a few questions and a clear step, as in [reply speed](/en/blog/turn-inquiries-into-bookings).

## Dealing with an angry or tense patient

1. **Listen to the end** without interrupting or defending.
2. **Acknowledge the feeling:** "I understand this is upsetting, and I apologize for the delay."
3. **Offer a specific solution** with a time, then carry it out.
4. **If it's beyond your authority,** hand it to the person in charge, politely.
5. **Record what happened** in their file so the team knows.

## What staff don't say

- They don't give a diagnosis or medical advice.
- They don't promise results or prices they have no authority to commit to.
- They don't reveal one patient's information to another person without permission.
- They don't say "I don't know" and stop, but "I'll check and come back to you within an hour."

## Training the team

Have the team practice these phrases by acting out short situations, and review real conversations together monthly to see what works. When ready-made replies are stored in the system, everyone uses them in the same wording.

Q: Must staff memorize the scripts word for word?
A: No. What's needed is the structure and the key phrases, in a natural voice that suits each patient.

Q: How do I reply to someone who only asks about price?
A: Explain that the price depends on their case, offer an exam appointment, and then state what the price includes after the diagnosis.

Q: Should I use the same reply for every patient on WhatsApp?
A: Use ready replies as a base, but change the name and details, because patients notice copied messages.
